The material

Corners

A lead-in cuts a short tapered slit beyond the corner. Auto keeps it off parts: outlines lead in on their outside, holes inside a part on their inside. Open paths have no inside or outside, so they swivel unless “Nothing” is chosen. Corners that lead in don’t also lift.

Offset test: find your blade offset

Cuts a row of test shapes in scrap, each with a different blade offset around yours, numbered by short ticks under them. Every corner swivels (no lead-ins), where a wrong offset shows plainly, and each tile gets a ramp of 2 offsets. Cut it, peel it, and pick the tile with the sharpest corners. X0 Y0 on the scrap’s front left corner, Z0 on its top.

A first guess for large blades: with the spindle’s power off, lower the tip onto paper and turn the blade a full turn by hand. Its tip draws a circle around the holder’s centre: the offset is half its diameter (12 mm across: 6 mm). See Help.

Which tile cut best?
Too small: corners rounded, the point blunt. Too big: little hooks past each corner. Right: square corners and a sharp point. Count the ticks under it; tile 0 at the left (if you cut it) has none and is uncorrected.
